## Part 1: ELM Terms of Use for Students

Your lecturers do not have access to your chat history in ELM for the purposes of 'checking' your work - like your email account, it is private to you.

### Using generative AI in your studies: guidelines for students

This guidance is for all students enrolled at the University of Edinburgh. It is University-level guidance, which means that it explains what uses of generative AI are not allowed anywhere in the University. You will also have access to guidance from your programme and from your courses, which will have more specific information relevant to each assignment. This guidance may allow you to use AI in certain ways, or it may restrict it further. If you are not clear about what you are allowed to do with AI on your course, speak to your lecturer, tutor or course organiser.

AI Essentials short course for Edinburgh students: free, via Learn (Essentials link in the left hand menu).

**Overview.** The University recognises that developing skills in the responsible use of generative AI is important. It trusts you to act responsibly and aims to give clarity on when use breaches the rules on academic misconduct.

**Unacceptable uses of generative AI for assessment.** Passing off someone's - or something's - work as your own is academic misconduct (e.g. failing to cite a source, getting an AI agent to complete your work, claiming authorship of machine-generated content, presenting machine-translated work as your own). You risk investigation under the academic misconduct procedures. The following are misconduct:

1. Presenting AI outputs as your own, original work.
2. Using an AI translator to convert assessed work to English before submission (treated as false authorship).
3. Submitting an assessment which includes elements of AI-generated text without acknowledgment.
4. Submitting an assessment which includes AI-generated images, audio or video without acknowledgment.
5. Submitting an assessment which includes AI-generated mathematical formulae or reasoning, or computer code, without acknowledgment.
6. Citing and referencing AI-found sources without reading and verifying them.
7. Using an AI agent or AI browser to complete any type of work - assessed or not - within your virtual learning environment.

**Live translation apps.** Do not use third-party AI-based translation apps in class unless you have a schedule of adjustments that specifies this. Outside the classroom you may use translation services to clarify notes, but only the University's generative AI service (ELM) for this, as it has in-built privacy protections.

**AI agents.** Using an AI agent within Learn or any other University learning or assessment platform that requires login is not permitted. Sharing login details with an agent or agentic browser breaches the University Computing Acceptable Use Policy and will result in removal of access to digital services and disciplinary action.

**Using ELM.** All students have free access to ELM, a secure gateway to a range of models, including locally-hosted, energy-efficient models like Qwen, and larger models like Claude and GPT. ELM keeps data secure (not used by third parties to train models), is free, gives free choice of model, and your interactions cannot be monitored by lecturers.

**Reasons for caution.** Over-dependence on AI can harm learning (cognitive offloading); training and running AI has environmental costs; output can be biased, fabricated or wrong, and you are accountable for errors you include in assessed work; there are concerns about AI ownership and governance.

**Acknowledging and citing AI use.** Follow your course's guidance on how to acknowledge use of generative AI in each assignment. Content generated by AI within your work needs an in-text citation or footnote and a reference-list entry (see the Library's guide to using generative AI).

### Terms of Use for ELM

ELM is provided by the University to safeguard University data and give all staff and students equitable access to advanced generative AI tools. It provides the University's self-hosted Llama 3.3 model and OpenAI's model family.

- ELM uses generative AI, which carries ethical concerns and risks (deepfakes, privacy violations, disinformation, misleading content, bias). Keep a critical perspective and be circumspect about the data you send.
- ELM operates under a Zero Data Retention policy with OpenAI; data are limited to University use. The self-hosted Llama model gives additional assurances and is the recommended choice. For personal or private data contact edina@ed.ac.uk. ELM must not be used with identifiable patient or clinical data.
- Familiarise yourself with OpenAI's Service Terms, Sharing and Publication Policy, and Usage Policies.
- Feedback is anonymous. Certain content (e.g. inappropriate language) is flagged on entry; read the Acceptable Use Policy and Privacy Notice.
- ELM abides by the University's Use of Operational Data policy and its guidance on generative AI.
- **All activity with ELM is logged for evaluation, linked to your UUN and other personal data (IP address, email address).**
- The site uses cookies (see the Cookies policy).

## Part 2: API key request agreement

By requesting a key you confirm:

- You have familiarised yourself with the Code of Practice for the Cyber Security of AI and the University-specific guidance for working with generative AI.
- Any key issued will only be used by the intended recipient(s), as indicated in the request or communicated to EDINA, and solely for legitimate University of Edinburgh business.
- You have read and agree to the Guidelines on how to use and secure your API key, and will keep it secure.
- You have access to your University email account and will monitor it for communications from EDINA about your usage.
- You receive one key, valid for one year. Keys inactive for more than 2 months are revoked.
- **Keys are limited to $500 annually.** A Python library is available to monitor usage and model prices are published. EDINA will email you when you approach the limit. Exceeding it immediately disables API access; exceptions need a request to the ELM team via Unidesk.
